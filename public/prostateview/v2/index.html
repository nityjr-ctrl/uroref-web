Behind the models

How MRI slices become a 3D teaching model.

Tap through the pipeline from public MRI data to checked, phone-ready 3D anatomy. Tap or click the technical words for plain-English explanations.

The problem

2D slices → mental 3D reconstruction

The hard part is building the gland in your head.

Axial MRI is flat, but prostate anatomy is spatial. Learners have to imagine apex, base, zones, urethra and lesion position from a stack of slices.

ProstateView makes that mental conversion visible: the source slice stays important, then the same anatomy becomes a 3D teaching model you can rotate.

Step 1

Prostate158 public dataset

Start with open, expert-annotated MRI.

The source set is Prostate158: 158 expert-annotated biparametric 3T prostate MRI examinations from Charite Berlin, acquired on Siemens VIDA and Skyra scanners.

It includes , and maps. The prostate zones and lesion labels were annotated by expert radiologists. Source: Prostate158

Step 2

NIfTI segmentation mask

Use the outline as the ground truth shape.

Expert prostate outlines are stored as a segmentation mask. Each slice contributes one piece of the final volume.

Stacked together, those masks form a rough 3D object. It is accurate to the source, but still looks like an organ built from tiny blocks.

Step 3

Gaussian blur + marching cubes

Turn blocky voxels into a continuous surface.

The binary mask is gently antialiased with a , then surfaced with .

The technical detail matters: extracting the 0.5 isosurface keeps the model close to the source mask before any visual smoothing happens.

Step 4

Taubin smoothing

Smooth without quietly shrinking the gland.

Simple smoothing can make a mesh smaller, especially around the apex and base. That would make the model look cleaner but less faithful.

reduces staircase roughness while preserving the prostate volume much better than a plain Laplacian pass.

Step 5

Largest connected component

Keep one clean prostate body.

Raw masks can contain disconnected specks or islands from contour noise. Those should not appear as anatomy in the teaching model.

The pipeline keeps the main connected prostate shape, drops tiny noise, and flags ambiguous cases for human review rather than hiding them.

Step 6

Dice + HD95 + volume delta

Check the mesh against the source.

A smooth model is not enough. Each mesh is compared back to the original mask for overlap, surface distance and volume.

Current teaching QA: 97 of 100 meet the teaching standard. The remaining contour-review cases are documented before release as teaching assets.

Step 7

Decimation + GLB export

Make it light enough for a phone.

The validated mesh is simplified only after QA, then exported as a web-friendly model for browser viewing and phone handoff.

The point is not to ship the heaviest possible mesh. It is to preserve the teaching anatomy while keeping interaction smooth.
