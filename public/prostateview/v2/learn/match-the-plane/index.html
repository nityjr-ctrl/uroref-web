Evidence base
  1. Spatial skill is trainable and predicts surgical performance (laparoscopy, robotics). - Sagoo et al., Anat Sci Educ 2025; Cohen & Hegarty.
  2. Active 3D manipulation beats passive viewing, and helps low-spatial learners most. - Sanandaji et al., 2020.
  3. Free viewpoint can overwhelm novices, so constrain it early and release it later. - Sanandaji et al., 2020.
  4. Photorealism raises cognitive load; clean colour-coded models lower it. - Koh, Tan & Mogali, Sci Rep 2023.
  5. Interleave contrasting categories, track speed and accuracy, retire mastered types. - Kellman, Krasne & Massey (perceptual-adaptive learning).
  6. Interleaving improves discrimination between look-alike categories (d about 0.67). - Pan et al., 2019.
  7. Add one difficulty axis at a time; over-stacking can reduce learning. - Larsen et al., Medical Teacher 2022.
  8. Immediate corrective feedback after each item supports durable learning. - Bjork & Bjork (desirable difficulties).
  9. Novice prostate-MRI readers gain rapidly up to about 40 cases. - Rosenkrantz et al., AJR 2017.
  10. Competence plateaus around 69 to 75 cases for radiology and urology trainees. - Eur Radiol 2025.
