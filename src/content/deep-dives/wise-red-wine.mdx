I get asked this on most post-op ward rounds. A man in his sixties, the morning after his TURP, wants to know if he can have his usual glass of red with dinner. I've always said "best leave it for a bit". I had nothing to back that up. Turns out nobody did.

The San Raffaele group in Milan went and randomised it. They took 132 people having elective urology, mostly endoscopic, who already drank 7 to 21 glasses a week. A psychologist screened them first to keep out problem drinking. Half were told to stop. The other half were handed 150 mL of red with lunch and again with dinner for 30 days after surgery, measured out by the investigators and never within six hours of an opioid.

The primary outcome was mental wellbeing on the SF-36 at a month. **No difference.** Complications ran at 16.1% in the abstinence arm and 9.8% with wine, all minor and not significant. There were five readmissions in the abstinence arm and none with wine, which the authors put down to chance and case mix. I'd agree.

Now the catch. Only 123 patients were analysed, nobody was blinded, 98% were men, and it's one centre. The wine was donated by a producer that also funds the institute's research. Two glasses a day comes to about 27 UK units a week, nearly double the guideline, and the authors are clear the trial can't prove it's safe.

So that's my "best leave it for a bit" exposed as habit, not evidence. Gosh, and I was so looking forward to writing "Merlot PRN" on the drug chart. One trial, one centre, and the wine came from a sponsor, so the advice stays the same for now. Just with slightly less conviction.
